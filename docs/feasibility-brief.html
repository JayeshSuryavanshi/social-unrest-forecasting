Can we actually predict social unrest?

Reviving a 2022 masters project on ACLED conflict data — an honest audit of what was real, what the field can actually do, and the version worth building now.

ACLED live · updated through Jun 2026 GDELT pulled today, no key Bing News API retired Aug 2025 Original "85% prediction" label leakage

The bottom line

Yes — but not the thing the original project claimed to do.

The idea is sound and the data is real. But "predicting unrest" splits into three very different problems, and they are not equally solvable. The original project's headline result measured none of them — it recovered a label it had built from the same text it was predicting from. Reframed as a genuine out-of-time forecast, this is a legitimate, feasible, portfolio-grade project. The catch: much of the achievable "accuracy" is just conflict's stubborn persistence, and beating that baseline is the whole game.

The risk map

Which regions are dangerous over the next 1–24 months. Done operationally today. Modest-to-good skill.

Will active violence persist / roughly how intense. Predictable — but a naive "same as last month" already nails most of it.

✕ NOT PREDICTION — circular label leakage

Why the 0.85 isn't what it looks like

The number is a mirror, not a forecast

Confirmed by reading the original notebooks directly — the classifiers print accuracy 0.8504672897196262. Here is the exact loop that produced it:

1 · Input

A news article's own words & keywords (via newspaper3k).

→

2 · Label built from…

the same text: label = 1 if its TF-IDF cosine similarity to any ACLED note ≥ 0.30.

→

3 · Model learns

to recover that label from the same words. Random 90/10 split, no future held out.

The circularity: the target is a deterministic function of the input's own vocabulary. The model just rediscovers that "articles about protests contain protest words." 0.85 measures internal consistency of a keyword rule — not whether news anticipates events. The "~5-day warning lead time" is the same artifact: the code only keeps article–event pairs where the article predates the event, so a positive gap is guaranteed by construction.

A real forecast predicts an outcome that exists in the world independent of the document. Here, the future is never referenced — so you can't be right or wrong about it.

The persistence trap — internalize this

Political violence is extraordinarily sticky in space and time: violent last month → almost certainly violent next month; calm stays calm. So a "no-change" baseline is a brutally high bar. In the ViEWS escalation-prediction competition across 15+ teams, no model beat no-change on the test set under its own metric. Implication: any honest result is reported as skill over persistence — a few points of AUPRC — never as a raw accuracy headline. And new-onset in calm areas (base rate often <1–3%) is dramatically harder than continuation, by near-consensus in the literature.

The version worth building

A genuine, feasible reformulation

Forecast a future ACLED outcome from strictly past information — a real out-of-time task with a checkable answer. ACLED is ground truth; news is only a feature, never the label.

The task

For each state (ACLED admin1) and week, predict the probability of an unrest event (protest or violence) there next period. India's 37 states are the testbed — heterogeneous enough (calm vs hot) that history features carry signal. Freeze the label definition before modeling.

Non-negotiable protocol

  • Out-of-time only. Train on the past, test on a strictly-future block. Rolling-origin backtest, like ViEWS — never a random split.
  • Beat the baselines or report that you didn't: persistence, per-unit climatology, and a conflict-history GBDT with no text.
  • Right metrics: AUPRC (with base rate), Brier + calibration, lead-time decay (t+1 vs t+3), all as skill-over-persistence. Accuracy is meaningless here.

Phased scope — laptop-scale

P3

The NLP / LLM lane

Add a GDELT / ACLED-notes / LLM text-signal stream. Measure marginal AUPRC lift over the history model.

out → does news add early-warning value?

The honest, interesting question — and a genuine contribution: does open-source news text add forecasting skill beyond conflict history, and at what lead time? A credible "not much, once history is controlled" is a real result. A recycled 0.85 is not.

The GDELT method-validation (real-time, 18 months)

Before ACLED, I proved the pipeline on 18 months of GDELT for India (37 states, 3.1M events) — leakage-free panel, rolling-origin backtest, unrest per state per week.

Two labels, two questions. Occurrence = "any unrest next week" (base rate 0.82, so AUROC is the lens). Escalation = "unrest spikes above the state's own recent norm" (base rate 0.14 — the hard, valuable question, so AUPRC is the lens). GDELT is a noisy news proxy, not ACLED — this validates the method, not a deployable model.

Contrast — Nigeria (same harness, first testbed): base rate 0.96, so "any violence" is near-trivial and on escalation the model does not beat persistence at t+1 (−0.008) — a clean reproduction of ViEWS's "no model beats no-change." The headline experiment: does news text add anything?

This is your 2022 question, asked honestly. I split the features into event history vs news text (coverage tone + article volume) and measured the marginal value of adding text on top of history — same rolling-origin backtest.

News text adds essentially nothing once conflict history is controlled — and text alone is worse than history alone. The exact inverse of the recycled 0.85, and a genuine, publishable negative result.

Why this is the *right* answer, not a disappointment: it matches the literature exactly — Mueller & Rauh show newspaper text helps mainly for onset in low-history regions, and every Indian state has rich event history, so a shallow news signal has nothing to add.

The follow-up: does text help where history is sparse?

India is high-history everywhere. The literature says text should help most where history is absent. So I pulled six normally-calmer countries and re-ran the ablation across the base-rate spectrum — from Ecuador (14% of province-weeks see unrest) up to saturated Kenya (96%).

The hypothesis is only faintly supported: text-only overtakes history-only in exactly one place — the lowest-history country — but the marginal value of text on top of history is ≈0 everywhere (−0.03 to +0.01). Shallow news signal and event history are largely redundant.

The honest, narrow claim: a shallow news signal (coverage tone + article volume) adds no marginal value beyond event history, even where history is sparse — though it does start to stand in for history at the extreme low end. Whether deep text clears that bar was the next experiment — below.

Deep text: GKG themes — the faithful version

The real test uses GDELT's GKG thematic tags (PROTEST, UNREST, political-violence, terror… parsed from full article text) — the open analog to the news-topic models Mueller & Rauh used. GKG is too sparse at province level for small countries, so I ran it on India (2.9M article-mentions, 45 theme features, dense).

Occurrence · t+10.9900.991+0.001
Escalation · t+10.1730.173−0.002
Escalation · t+30.1770.196 ↑+0.004

Deep text is a clear step up from shallow: GKG-only now matches history on occurrence and even beats it on escalation at t+3 (0.196 vs 0.177). Richer text carries as much signal as the coded event stream. But it still adds ≈0 on top of history (−0.002 to +0.004).

Why every text experiment nets to zero marginal: in a GDELT-only pipeline the "event history" is itself news-derived — GDELT events are coded from the same articles GKG themes tag. History and text are two views of one source, so text can't add orthogonal signal.

Toward closing the loop. Mueller & Rauh got a positive text result because their history came from a curated dataset that is genuinely empty in low-history countries, leaving room for text. The faithful test needs a non-news history source. ACLED is a step toward that — human-curated, higher-fidelity — but (per the review below) still ~77% newspaper-sourced, so it's curated news, not fully independent ground truth.

A 4-agent adversarial review re-ran this and forced 3 corrections — I verified each

  • "Text significantly degrades the forecast" was wrong — an unregularized-RF artifact (92 features, ~136 positives). Under L2/L1 logistic regression the t+3 marginal flips positive (+0.024 to +0.029). Text carries a little signal; it just can't beat the best history model.
  • "ACLED is independent of news" was false — ~77% of ACLED India events are newspaper-sourced. It's human-curated news, not non-news ground truth, so the "circularity confirmed" framing is overstated. A clean test needs truly non-news (administrative) history — never run.
  • Coverage: 33 of 36 states (Telangana, Ladakh, Sikkim dropped for missing FIPS codes) — scope the claim to that subset.

The corrected verdict across every text experiment — shallow and deep, 8 countries 0.14→0.96 base rate, GDELT + ACLED history: news text does not improve on the best history-only forecaster of unrest. It is at most weakly informative and redundant with the event record for the best model. Whether text helps over a truly non-news structural baseline is the real open question — untested here.

Getting the data in 2026

Access, changed since your course

GDELT — works right now, no key

The free, redistributable replacement for the retired Bing News API. Verified live today: one 15-min slice returned 162 unrest events (protests in Delhi; fighting concentrated in Iran, UK, US). Raw 15-minute CSVs, plain public URLs, no registration. Safe to commit (with citation).

ACLED — free, but re-plumbed

Still current (weekly releases, through mid-2026; it weathered the 2025 US funding cuts via UN CRAF'd funding). But the old email+key scheme is dead — now OAuth2 bearer tokens via a myACLED account. Raw-event API access wants an institutional email; a personal Gmail lands the most limited tier. Never commit ACLED data — their terms forbid redistribution.
